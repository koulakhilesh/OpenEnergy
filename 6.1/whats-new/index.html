    What's new¶

    6.1: the web app becomes a simulator¶

    OpenEnergy 6.1 changes only the web app and its data; the library, the API and every model result are as in 6.0.

    • One simulator instead of pages. open-energy-eight.vercel.app opens on Britain's grid hour by hour: each fuel flows into the GB node with a band as wide as its output, the node shows the day-ahead price, and your battery charges and discharges on a plan the optimiser makes in the browser. Play at 1, 4 or 24 hours a second, step with the arrow keys, or click a day on the year strip (each day coloured by its price spread). A side panel sets the battery, the forecast it plans with and the day, and shows the day and the whole year for all three forecasts.
    • Four tabs: Simulator, Play, 2035 and Research. Play lets you paint a day's plan, then run the day and watch actual prices and the scores come in; it keeps the engine's rule that the battery starts and ends the day half full. 2035 counts the saturation study's value up and down as you add batteries. Research holds the analyst pages (/run, /result, /explore, /compare, /scenarios), unchanged.
    • Revenue after wear in the browser. The 6.0 Battery Lab compared revenue before wear, so on days when a forecast's plan traded more it could show more than perfect foresight (on 20 October 2026 a 1 MW / 2 MWh battery on OpenEnergy's forecast showed 113%). The simulator now compares revenue less wear, the quantity the optimiser maximises, so perfect foresight is the most any plan earns (90% that day). The Python backtest and the API's year results are unchanged.
    • 2026 on the grid. NESO's generation mix now runs to 29 October 2026; the rows to 2025 are unchanged, and the system model still uses 2015–2025.
    • Addresses. /lab and /replay open the simulator with their day and battery, so 6.0 links keep working; sources are one click away under each view. The app and the docs use the README's logo.

    6.0¶

    OpenEnergy 6.0 makes the model more accurate, answers a research question, and puts the battery optimiser in the browser. Three defaults changed, and one bug was fixed that moves results; this section lists each with what it does to the numbers, measured by re-running every result in these docs.

    New¶

    • A day-ahead forecast built on fundamentals (forecast: fundamentals): NESO's day-ahead demand forecast, Elexon's day-ahead wind forecast, the daily gas price and recent prices, each only as published by the decision time, in a model retrained every month. From 2023 it halves the error of last week's prices, and a 1 MW / 2 MWh battery planning on it keeps 86–89% of the perfect-foresight revenue instead of 74–81% (Research). The forecast for every hour since 2019 is bundled (data/forecasts/).
    • How much storage can GB use? openenergy system saturation scales each FES 2035 battery fleet from none to three times FES's figure on a secure system: the first half of the fleet takes half or more of a battery's value away, and each MW of FES batteries replaces only 0.09–0.16 MW of firm capacity in a cold, still spell (Forward scenarios).
    • The optimiser in your browser: the web app gains a live home page, the Battery Lab (any day, battery and forecast, with the whole year solved in the background), Grid replay, the 2035 study and Beat the battery. They run the same daily programme in TypeScript on HiGHS compiled to WebAssembly, tested against the Python backtest. Concepts has a widget to drag prices and watch the plan.
    • A reinforcement-learning environment (openenergy.rl, pip install 'openenergy[rl]'): a Gymnasium environment with the optimiser as the baseline. An agent trained in the design work kept 27–38% of perfect-foresight revenue where the optimiser kept 78–83%, so "forecast, then optimise" stays (Research).
    • Data: the daily System Average Price of gas (ONS), NESO's day-ahead demand forecast and Elexon's day-ahead wind forecast, each with issue times (data/fundamentals/, openenergy data update --fundamentals; Data).

    Daily gas. The backcast follows the gas crisis much better: the merit order's error falls by 31–42% in 2021–2024 and its correlation with actual prices rises from 0.40 to 0.81 in 2022 and from 0.10 to 0.53 in 2024. Before the crisis the change is mixed (the 2019 mean is £4.2 lower, now £4.8 below actual), and the missing daily spread is unchanged (System model). FES results and every system scenario from 2018 change with it.

    Auction information. Last week's prices, perfect foresight and the gradient-boosting forecaster never used the previous afternoon's prices, so their results do not change. The fundamentals forecaster uses them.

    Secure FES systems. Holistic Transition, Electric Engagement and Hydrogen Evolution 2035 get 6.25–9.25 GW of firm capacity, and their unserved energy falls from 215–715 GWh to 7–13 GWh. Their mean prices fall from £120–211/MWh to £73–76/MWh, and a battery's perfect-foresight value on them from 1.8–2.7 times the model's own 2025 to about it or below (Forward scenarios).

    Fixed: the end-of-day energy target¶

    The daily plan required the battery to end each day with at least the energy it began with. When the last hour was cheap, a plan could end fuller, and the next day's target was then that fuller level, so the battery kept ratcheting up and holding energy it could have sold. The plan now ends exactly at the target (\(E_T = E_{target}\), Concepts).

    The effect depends on how often the last hour is cheap on the forecast:

    • Last week's prices: under 0.3% a year (2017: £20,682 to £20,633 per MW-year; 2019: £13,936 to £13,943).
    • Revenue stacking: up to 2% (2025 with DC, DM and DR, energy unpaid: £89,807 to £90,766; with energy at day-ahead prices, £127,976 to £125,634).
    • Re-planning every half-hour on imbalance prices, where each re-plan set a new target: the result changed sign in three of four years, from losing 6–39% against day-ahead to +18%, +17%, +1% and −12% (Revenue stacking).
    • The fundamentals forecast in its design stage: a 2024 capture ratio of 73.7% became 85.3%.

    The web app¶

    The year form moved from / to /run; /result, /explore, /compare and /scenarios keep their addresses, so shared links still work. The app offers the fundamentals forecast for historic years from 2019, and the FES table shows the firm capacity each pathway needed.

    Numbers re-run¶

    Every table and number in these docs was re-run for 6.0 with the commands and scripts named next to it. The ones that moved by more than rounding are on the pages linked above; the Research and Revenue stacking tables move by under 2% except where noted.

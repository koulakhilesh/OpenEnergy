    Data¶

    Bundled extract¶

    data/time_series/time_series_60min_singleindex_filtered.csv is an hourly extract of the Open Power System Data time-series package covering Great Britain from 31 December 2014 to 30 September 2020. It contains the GB day-ahead price (in GBP/MWh), load, and solar and wind generation and capacity. Timestamps are UTC (utc_timestamp). Version 2020-10-06 is still OPSD's latest time-series release (checked 13 October 2026), so later years come from the sources below.

    Of 2,101 days, 2,085 have a complete set of prices. The gaps are two-hour holes on the spring clock change each year, two single missing hours, and four outages of about a day. With the default max_gap_hours: 2, the short gaps are interpolated and the outages are skipped.

    Prices include genuine scarcity spikes, up to £999/MWh in September–November 2016; they are kept.

    GB prices after 2020¶

    OPSD's GB prices stop in September 2020. data/prices/gb_day_ahead_ember.csv carries the GB hourly day-ahead price from 30 June 2016 to 28 October 2026 (90,553 hours, no gaps), from Ember's European wholesale price data (CC BY 4.0), stored in EUR/MWh as published. OpenEnergy converts each hour to GBP at the ECB reference rate for its day (data/prices/ecb_gbp_per_eur.csv; the latest earlier rate on weekends and holidays). Use it with data: {source: ember, path: ...}; openenergy data update downloads the latest months into a user cache.

    Where both exist (July 2016 to September 2020, 37,239 hours) the converted Ember price matches OPSD with correlation 0.99996 and a mean absolute difference of £0.11/MWh, so the two can be joined. The system model's backcast does exactly that: OPSD where it has a price, Ember otherwise.

    Year 2017 2018 2019 2020 2021 2022 2023 2024 2025
    Mean £/MWh 45.3 57.4 42.9 35.3 117.8 204.8 94.1 72.5 80.7
    Mean daily spread £/MWh 41.6 40.6 32.9 39.6 146.4 155.6 70.0 59.9 67.5

    The series keeps 615 negative hours (down to −€60/MWh) and 38 hours at €1,000/MWh or more (up to €2,576/MWh in 2021–2022). From 2021 Ember takes the UK price from EMR Settlement, whose own reuse terms could not be read (the site blocks scripted access); OpenEnergy relies on Ember's CC BY 4.0 publication and cites both.

    Neighbouring markets¶

    data/prices/europe_day_ahead_ember.csv holds the hourly day-ahead prices of the six markets GB's interconnectors reach (France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Norway, Denmark and Ireland), EUR/MWh as published by Ember, from 1 January 2015 (Ireland from 1 October 2018) to 30 October 2026. The system model's interconnector links trade at these prices, converted to GBP like the GB price.

    Mean £/MWh (ECB rates) 2019 2020 2021 2022 2023 2024 2025
    GB 42.9 35.3 117.8 204.8 94.1 72.5 80.7
    France 34.6 28.7 93.4 235.1 84.4 48.9 52.2
    Netherlands 36.1 28.7 88.1 206.2 83.5 65.3 74.3
    Belgium 34.5 28.4 89.1 208.4 84.7 59.4 70.6

    Denmark 34.2 23.5 75.4 184.0 73.7 59.8 69.9

    From 2023 to 2025 GB's mean price was above every neighbour's except Ireland's, which helps explain why price-taking links at full capacity import so much (each link's availability is therefore fitted from its flows; see below).

    Known issues: Ember publishes one price for Norway and one for Denmark, without saying how the bidding zones are combined; GB's links land in southern Norway (NO2) and western Denmark (DK1). A few hours are blank on 31 July and 30 September 2026 (none before 2026).

    Renewable capacity factors¶

    The extract also has hourly capacity-factor profiles (generation divided by installed capacity) for solar, wind, wind_onshore and wind_offshore, from 2015 to 29 December 2019; OPSD stops publishing capacity after that.

    In 233 hours the solar profile exceeds 1.0 (up to 1.367): reported capacity lags new build, so generation divided by capacity overshoots. OpenEnergy clips profiles to [0, 1] and reports how many values were clipped. The same lag pushes GB solar capacity factors to 15–18%, above the roughly 11% usually quoted; this changes absolute output but not capture rates, which depend on the shape of the profile.

    Other OPSD zones can be used by pointing data.path at a larger OPSD time_series_*_singleindex.csv. OPSD publishes GB prices in GBP and all other zones in EUR; OpenEnergy labels the currency accordingly.

    Carbon intensity¶

    data/carbon_intensity/gb_national.csv holds GB national carbon intensity for every half-hour from 1 January 2018 to 29 October 2026: NESO's forecast and its estimated actual, in gCO2/kWh. It was downloaded with scripts/fetch_carbon_intensity.py and is stored exactly as published; 179 half-hours the API did not return are left blank.

    The archive has 804 half-hours without an actual value (18 runs, the longest about three days) and 16 implausible forecasts (up to 13,579 gCO2/kWh). OpenEnergy treats any value outside 0–1000 gCO2/kWh as missing and reports how many it rejected. Annual mean actual intensity falls from 248 (2018) to 214 (2019), 180 (2020), 188 (2021), 183 (2022), 152 (2023), 125 (2024), 129 (2025) and 122 gCO2/kWh (2026 to 12 October).

    These are average intensities of the whole system. Storage and renewables change the marginal plant, whose emissions can differ; emissions results in OpenEnergy show direction and rough scale, not causal impact.

    GB system data¶

    data/system/, written by scripts/fetch_system_data.py, feeds the system model:

    File Contents Source
    generation_mix.csv Half-hourly GB generation by fuel, MW, 2015-01-01 to 2025-12-31 (192,864 rows, no gaps) NESO historic generation mix
    fleet.csv GB capacity at each year end 2014–2025 (CCGT, coal, gas turbines, oil engines, nuclear, pumped storage) and CCGT and coal efficiency DUKES 5.8 and 5.10
    fuel_prices.csv Quarterly coal, oil and gas prices paid by major power producers, 2014–2025; the model's gas price before 2018 and with gas_price: quarterly DESNZ QEP 3.2.1
    carbon_prices.csv EU ETS (2015–2020) and UK ETS (2021–2025) price on 1 April with ECB exchange rates, and carbon price support rates World Bank, DESNZ (2021), ECB, HMRC
    unit_parameters.csv Unit size, minimum stable output and start-up cost for CCGT, coal and peaking plant (unit commitment) Danish Energy Agency, NREL, ECB
    interconnectors.csv GB's ten built interconnectors: neighbour, import and export capacity (MW) NESO Interconnector Register, 12 October 2026
    interconnector_flows.csv Daily imports and exports per link, MWh, 2016-01-01 to 2026-10-29 Elexon BMRS FUELHH
    interconnector_flows_hourly.csv Hourly net flow per link, MW, 2019-01-01 to 2026-10-29 Elexon BMRS FUELHH
    interconnector_availability.csv Each link's import and export availability: actual flow over a price taker's trade on actual prices, 2019–2025 Elexon BMRS FUELHH, Ember, OPSD, ECB
    interconnector_gap_availability.csv Each link's share of capacity used by size of the price gap (option availability: gap), 2019–2025 Elexon BMRS FUELHH, Ember, OPSD, ECB

    The model's inputs stop at the latest complete calendar year, 2025 (checked 13 October 2026): DUKES publishes a year's fleet the following July, so 2026 has none yet, and the 2026 fuel and carbon prices that exist are not added until the year is complete, so every model year has the same kind of inputs.

    Known issues: NESO fills gaps by seasonal decomposition and sets net-negative values to zero, so pumping demand and net exports are absent; transmission solar and batteries are in "other". DUKES efficiencies are UK-wide averages. One ETS price per year misses moves within the year (EU ETS 2018 rose from about €10 to €25; UK ETS 2021–2023 swung more). The World Bank dashboard has no UK ETS price on 1 April 2021, before the first auction, so 2021 uses DESNZ's 2021 carbon price (mean auction price, £47.96/t). DESNZ suppresses some coal prices from 2023 (carried forward) and gives no coal efficiency for 2025 (latest earlier value used). Quarterly fuel prices are what power producers paid, including contracts agreed earlier, so they lag market gas prices; since v6.0 the model uses the daily wholesale gas price from 2018 instead (below, and the system model). Emission factors (gas 0.1839, coal 0.3096 tCO2 per MWh of fuel) follow from the carbon price support rates, which are set at £18/tCO2.

    The interconnector register holds today's capacities only. Each link's first day in service comes from its flows (the first flow in the first 14 days carrying at least a fifth of its capacity, which skips commissioning tests): Nemo Link 2019-01-30, IFA2 2021-01-22, NS Link 2021-09-23, ElecLink 2022-05-23, Viking Link 2023-12-29 and Greenlink 2025-01-19. The flows also show limits the register does not (Moyle about 300 MW in 2016–2017, IFA about half its capacity in 2022, BritNed never above about 1,050 of 1,200 MW), so modelled link capacity is overstated in those periods.

    Each link's availability (scripts/fit_interconnector_availability.py) is the energy it actually moved (Elexon) over what a price taker would have moved at full capacity on actual GB and neighbour day-ahead prices, per direction, capped at 1. It uses actual prices and flows only. Fitted on 2019–2022 it gives 36.9 TWh of imports in 2024 (actual 43.9, full capacity 66.0) and 11.8 TWh of exports (actual 10.6); the published file is fitted on 2019–2025.

    What is known the day before¶

    data/fundamentals/, written by scripts/fetch_fundamentals.py (or openenergy data update --fundamentals), holds what is published before the day-ahead auction, each with the time it was issued so a backtest uses only what existed at its decision time:

    File Contents Source
    gas_sap_daily.csv Daily System Average Price of gas, p/kWh, 2018-01-01 to 2026-10-25 (3,220 days, no gaps) ONS, from National Gas Transmission
    neso_demand_day_ahead.csv NESO's day-ahead national demand forecast at its cardinal points, with issue times, 2018-01-02 to 2026-11-01 (58,792 points) NESO Day Ahead Demand Forecast
    elexon_wind_day_ahead.csv Elexon's hourly wind forecast, the latest issued by noon UTC the day before, 2018-01-02 to 2026-11-01 (76,970 hours) Elexon BMRS WINDFOR

    The system model uses the daily gas price from its first day (quarterly DESNZ prices before); the fundamentals forecast uses all three. Known issues: SAP is a wholesale price without the network charges power stations pay; NESO's demand forecast covers transmission demand and its published times fit best an hour earlier (used as published); NESO's own wind forecast archive keeps only the latest issue for each half-hour, often made on the day, so Elexon's is used. Details in data/fundamentals/README.md.

    data/forecasts/gb_fundamentals_day_ahead.csv is model output, not source data: the fundamentals forecast for every hour from 2019 to 28 October 2026, exactly as a backtest would make it, so scenarios and the web app can use it without training (data/forecasts/README.md; built by scripts/build_fundamentals_forecast.py).

    Future Energy Scenarios¶

    data/fes/ holds NESO's Future Energy Scenarios tables unchanged, written by scripts/fetch_fes.py: the electricity supply (ES1) and demand summary (ED1) tables of FES: Pathways to Net Zero 2025 (pathways Holistic Transition, Electric Engagement, Hydrogen Evolution and Falling Behind, plus the Ten Year Forecast; 2024–2050) and of the 2026 Ten Year Outlook (2025–2036). system.fes uses their capacities (MW), storage (GWh) and annual system demand; see forward scenarios. FES 2025's 2025 column is already a projection and differs by pathway; FES publishes no fuel or carbon prices and no storage efficiencies.

    data/scenarios/ is model output, not source data: hourly modelled prices for each pathway in 2030 and 2035 on 2025's weather (fes_prices.csv) and a summary next to FES's own figures (fes_summary.csv), written by scripts/build_scenario_prices.py for the web app, and the storage saturation study (saturation.csv, saturation_years.csv, from openenergy system saturation; see forward scenarios). data/app/ is model output too: per-MW battery results for common requests (1, 2 and 4 hours, every year, forecast, response option and pathway), written by scripts/build_app_results.py so the app answers at once. web/public/data/ reshapes bundled data and these results into the static files the app's in-browser pages read (scripts/build_app_data.py), each carrying its attribution.

    Frequency response¶

    data/response/, written by scripts/fetch_response_data.py, feeds revenue stacking:

    • gb_dynamic_response.csv: the clearing price (GBP/MW/h) and cleared volume (MW) of each dynamic response service (DC, DM, DR; low and high) for every four-hour EFA block from 15 September 2021 22:00 to 30 October 2026 19:00 UTC (11,226 blocks, no gaps). DM and DR start on 26 March 2022.
    • gb_response_utilisation.csv: for each month from September 2021 to September 2026, the average share of contracted MW each service delivers (MWh per MW per hour), from NESO's one-second GB frequency and the delivery envelopes in NESO's Response Services Service Terms (version 6). The raw frequency files (about 75 MB a month) are not bundled.

    Known issues: Dynamic Containment before 16 September 2021 was bought pay-as-bid and is not included; some prices are negative (mostly DM and DR high); utilisation is the delivery of a unit following the envelope exactly. See data/response/README.md.

    Imbalance prices¶

    data/balancing/gb_system_prices.csv, written by scripts/fetch_system_prices.py, holds the GB system (imbalance) price for every half-hour from 2016 to 29 October 2026 (189,840 periods, 20 blank where Elexon's API returned nothing). GB has a single imbalance price, so the sell and buy prices are equal; it is used by examples/imbalance_value.py.

    Transmission constraints¶

    data/constraints/, written by scripts/fetch_constraints.py, holds the evidence on the constraints page; no model uses it yet: constraint_breakdown_daily.csv, NESO's daily constraint costs and volumes from April 2021 (values unchanged), and day_ahead_boundaries_monthly.csv, a monthly summary of NESO's half-hourly day-ahead limits and flows by boundary group from January 2023. Known issues are in data/constraints/README.md.

    Software¶

    The system model's storage dispatch uses PyPSA:

    T. Brown, J. Hörsch, D. Schlachtberger. PyPSA: Python for Power System Analysis. Journal of Open Research Software 6(1), 2018. https://doi.org/10.5334/jors.188.

    Optimisation problems are solved with HiGHS (MIT licence); the web app's in-browser optimiser uses highs-js, HiGHS compiled to WebAssembly (MIT licence).

    Each data folder has a README.md with the full source, licence and known issues, and every summary.json repeats the attribution for the data a run used.

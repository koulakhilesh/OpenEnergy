    Forward scenarios¶

    The system model rebuilds GB prices from the plants behind them. v4.0 adds two things that let it look forward, and v4.2 makes the links behave as they have and adds unit commitment to them:

    • Interconnector links (system.interconnectors: links): GB's ten interconnectors trade with France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Norway, Denmark and Ireland at those markets' actual day-ahead prices, instead of holding imports at their historic output.
    • NESO Future Energy Scenarios (system.fes): a FES pathway's capacities and demand for a year from 2025 to 2035, applied to a historic weather year.

    v6.0 makes each FES system secure to GB's reliability standard before valuing anything on it, and asks how much storage GB can use (below).

    uv sync --extra system
uv run openenergy system run examples/gb-2035-holistic.yaml

    name: gb-2035-holistic
system:
  data: ../data/system
  year: 2025                     # the weather year: weather, demand shape, fuel and carbon prices
  interconnectors: links         # or {mode: links, hurdle_gbp_per_mwh: 5, availability: 1.0}
  commitment: true               # start-up costs and minimum stable output (marginal price)
  fes:
    pathway: Holistic Transition # also Electric Engagement, Hydrogen Evolution, Falling Behind,
                                 # Ten Year Forecast (FES 2025) and Ten Year Outlook (2026)
    year: 2035                   # 2025 to 2035, not before the weather year
    battery_efficiency: 0.85     # round trip; FES publishes none (stated assumption)
    ldes_efficiency: 0.75        # round trip for new long-duration storage (stated assumption)
    adequacy: secure             # add the least firm capacity meeting lole_hours (v6.0); or as_modelled
    lole_hours: 3                # GB's reliability standard: hours a year with unserved energy
    # firm_mw: 8000              # or add exactly this much firm capacity, no search
    battery_scale: 1.0           # multiply the FES battery fleet's power
battery: {power_mw: 1, energy_mwh: 2}

    Results are "this fleet and demand in 2025's weather and markets", not price forecasts: FES publishes no fuel or carbon price paths, so the weather year keeps its own.

    Interconnector links¶

    Each built link in NESO's Interconnector Register is a price taker on GB's single bus: it imports up to its import capacity whenever GB's price is above the neighbour's actual day-ahead price (Ember, converted at ECB rates) plus a hurdle, and exports when GB's price is below the neighbour's minus the hurdle. A link joins the system on the day it entered service, read from Elexon's flows (Nemo Link 2019, IFA2 and NS Link 2021, ElecLink 2022, Viking Link at the end of 2023, Greenlink 2025). Historic imports are dropped. Irish prices start in October 2018, so links need a year from 2019. Links work with the merit order, PyPSA (when storage is added) and unit commitment.

    Setting Default Meaning
    mode fixed fixed: historic imports (as before v4.0); links: price-taking links
    hurdle_gbp_per_mwh 0 added to import prices and taken off export prices
    availability flows flows: each link's import and export share fitted from its actual flows (below); gap: a share that grows with the price gap, fitted from hourly flows (below); a number from 0 to 1 applies to every link
    prices prices/europe_day_ahead_ember.csv next to data neighbours' hourly prices

    At full capacity links trade far more than happened: imports up to 64% too high (2023) and exports up to 4.5 times too high (2021). A price taker sees every hour's price gap as profit; real flows are limited by outages, losses, ramping, capacity sold ahead, trading on later markets and the neighbours' own supply. Each link's availability (below) brings imports to within 5% of actual in 2022, 2024 and 2025; they are 10% high in 2023 and 17–29% low in 2019–2021. Exports are within 2% in 2022 and 10–22% low in 2023–2025, but about twice actual in 2019–2021: with the daily gas price the modelled GB price in those years is lower than actual, so the links export more and import less than they did.

    Matching flows takes spread away: links at full capacity were importing the neighbours' daily shape, which partly hid the model's own flat price (2025: 39.1 at full capacity, 23.8 at fitted availability). Unit commitment restores the shape on GB's own plant: the spread is 63% to 88% of actual in 2019–2020 and 2023–2025 (30% and 38% in the 2021–2022 gas crisis, even with the daily gas price). The mean is £4.5–4.9 below actual in 2019–2020, £2–6 above it in 2023–2025 and £19–29 below it in 2021–2022 (see the system model).

    Link availability¶

    scripts/fit_interconnector_availability.py sets each link's import and export availability to the energy it actually moved over what a price taker at full capacity would have moved on actual GB and neighbour prices, 2019–2025, capped at 1 (see Data). Fitted only on flows, never on prices. Fitted on 2019–2022 instead, it gives 36.9 TWh of imports in 2024 against 43.9 actual (66.0 at full capacity), and 11.8 TWh of exports against 10.6. It is one number per link and direction, so it does not respond to the size of the price gap.

    Availability by price gap (v5.0)¶

    Real links trade more of a bigger gap. From hourly flows (Elexon, 2019–2025) and actual prices, availability: gap takes each link's share of capacity in hours whose gap was £0–5, £5–10, £10–20, £20–40, £40–80 and over £80/MWh, made non-decreasing so each link can be offered as six steps, the k-th at the neighbour's price plus the bin's lower edge. Nemo Link, for example, imports 49% of its capacity at gaps under £5 and 90% above £80; IFA2 exports 12% and 78%. The Irish links barely respond. The shares are in data/system/interconnector_gap_availability.csv.

    Tested the same way as the flat share (both fitted on 2019–2022 flows and actual prices, links in service before 2023), it follows the hour-to-hour flows a little better and the yearly energy a little worse:

    Year Imports: actual / gap / flat Exports: actual / gap / flat Hourly correlation with actual flow: gap / flat
    2023 33.5 / 24.4 / 26.7 10.1 / 8.0 / 10.0 0.47 / 0.43
    2024 38.9 / 29.1 / 30.2 9.2 / 6.0 / 7.1 0.41 / 0.37
    2025 38.9 / 28.6 / 29.3 9.5 / 6.2 / 7.4 0.46 / 0.43

    In the model (links and unit commitment, 2023–2025) it moves the mean by under £1/MWh, lowers the daily spread by 2.1 to 3.4 (2025: 47.9 against 51.0), cuts exports by about a quarter, and changes the battery's perfect-foresight value by −10% to −7% (planning on last week's prices it earns 6% to 23% more). Because it does not beat the flat share out of sample, flows stays the default and gap is an option. It matters most for the FES pathways below, where links are short at scarcity.

    Marginal or start price¶

    With links and commitment, the marginal price is closer to history than the start price in 2023, 2024 and 2025 on the mean, the daily spread and battery value; the start price holds the night up at a running unit's cost plus its start cost. The scenarios below use the marginal price. This is a choice between two un-fitted definitions made on history. A 1 MW / 2 MWh battery (95% each way, £5/MWh wear), GBP per MW-year, last week's prices / perfect foresight (actual: the whole year; model: 358 or 359 days, from the second week):

    Year Actual Links Links + UC, marginal Links + UC, start
    2023 36,362 / 46,440 1,604 / 9,275 10,982 / 25,701 4,228 / 15,228
    2024 29,048 / 39,304 1,643 / 9,234 9,282 / 25,553 4,425 / 15,308
    2025 36,208 / 44,974 648 / 9,728 11,293 / 26,038 4,384 / 15,723

    With perfect foresight the model gives 55% to 65% of the actual value; planning on last week's prices, 30% to 32%.

    A hurdle (hurdle_gbp_per_mwh) is still available but left at 0.

    FES pathways¶

    system.fes scales the weather year's system by the ratio of the FES year to the weather year in the same pathway, so every half-hour keeps its weather and demand shape:

    Weather-year input Scaled by FES (target year / weather year)
    Wind (transmission and embedded) Offshore Wind + Onshore Wind capacity
    Solar Solar PV capacity
    Nuclear, hydro Nuclear; Hydro capacity
    Biomass Biomass + CCS Biomass capacity
    CCGT and peaking capacity (DUKES) Gas + CCS Gas + Hydrogen capacity
    Coal none (set to 0)
    Demand ED1 annual system demand
    Interconnector capacity Interconnectors capacity
    Batteries added in full: FES GW, hours = GWh / GW
    Long-duration storage added: FES increase over the weather year (GW, GWh)

    Pumped storage keeps its historic output. Hydrogen and CCS gas plant are costed as gas, because there is no open cost data for them. The weather year has to be a year the FES edition covers: from 2024 for FES 2025, from 2025 for the Ten Year Outlook. Any scale or capacity_mw you set overrides the FES value.

    A secure system first¶

    As FES's fleets stand, several pathways cannot meet demand through 2025's cold, still spell in January. GB buys capacity through the Capacity Market to a reliability standard of 3 hours' loss of load expectation a year (DESNZ), so since v6.0 a FES system is made secure first (fes.adequacy: secure, the default): the least firm peaking capacity, in 250 MW steps and costed as gas peaking plant, is added until hours with unserved energy are within fes.lole_hours (3). That is the "Firm added" column: 2.5 GW in Holistic Transition 2030; 6.25 to 9.25 GW in Holistic Transition, Electric Engagement and Hydrogen Evolution 2035; 0.25 GW in the Ten Year Outlook 2035; none elsewhere. A single weather year is one draw, not an expectation; the saturation study below averages two.

    fes.adequacy: as_modelled keeps the fleet as FES gives it, as before v6.0. Then six cases leave demand unserved, and the price sits at the £6,000/MWh value of lost load for much of it. Mean £/MWh and unserved GWh (hours with unserved energy), as modelled:

    Pathway and year Links as fitted (default) availability: gap Every link at full capacity
    Holistic Transition 2030 85.0, 68.0 (16) 80.2, 47.0 (14) 66.2, 0
    Holistic Transition 2035 210.5, 715.2 (81) 161.6, 508.0 (60) 84.9, 50.5 (8)
    Electric Engagement 2030 69.8, 9.7 (2) 60.1, 0 63.4, 0
    Electric Engagement 2035 186.9, 683.2 (81) 150.5, 480.4 (60) 77.6, 10.9 (3)
    Hydrogen Evolution 2035 119.9, 214.8 (30) 93.9, 150.1 (22) 64.1, 0
    Ten Year Outlook 2035 85.6, 17.1 (5) 58.5, 0 62.0, 0

    The cause is link availability. The fitted availability holds on GB's tightest days in the record: on the 1% of days with the highest GB price in 2019–2025, links imported 3% (ElecLink) to 42% (Nemo Link, BritNed) of their capacity, below their fitted shares for every link but East West (39% against 38%), because neighbours were often short at the same time. So derating links at stress is supported by the flows. What the model leaves out is what FES counts on in those hours: demand response, new flexibility and neighbours' own 2030s fleets. The secure top-up stands in for all of that with the cheapest firm capacity, so read it as "this much firm capacity, or its equivalent", not as a forecast of new gas plant.

    Hydrogen Evolution 2030 / 2035 5,860 / 5,145 17,514 / 22,031

    Compare pathways with the model's own 2025, not with actual 2025: on modelled 2025 prices the battery's perfect-foresight value is 58% of its actual value, and the model's daily spread is 76% of the real one. Against that baseline, every pathway's perfect-foresight value is about the same or lower: 2% lower in Holistic Transition 2035 and 2% higher in Electric Engagement 2030, 15% to 33% lower in Holistic Transition 2030, Electric Engagement and Hydrogen Evolution 2035, Hydrogen Evolution 2030 and the Ten Year Outlook 2035, and 39% to 64% lower in Falling Behind, the Ten Year Forecast and the Ten Year Outlook 2030. With secure systems the 2035 pathways no longer rise far above the baseline on days of lost load (before v6.0 they reached 1.8 to 2.7 times it), but scarcity still matters: with perfect foresight over the full year, the one or two days with an hour at £1,000/MWh or more earn 29% to 49% of the value in the six cases that have them (49% of Holistic Transition 2035's). Planning on last week's prices keeps 15% to 53% of the perfect-foresight value, against 81% on actual 2025 prices and 43% on the model's own 2025.

    How much storage can GB use?¶

    The batteries above join a fleet that FES has already sized: 23 to 33 GW of batteries in 2035 in FES 2025's pathways, storing 1.3 to 1.5 hours on average. What happens to the value of one more battery as that fleet grows, once the system is secure? openenergy system saturation scales each pathway's 2035 battery fleet from none to three times FES's figure (same duration), and for each fleet adds the least firm capacity that keeps loss of load, averaged over the 2024 and 2025 weather years, within the 3-hour standard. A 1 MW / 2 MWh battery (95% each way, £5/MWh wear) is then valued with perfect foresight on each year's modelled prices (data/scenarios/saturation.csv; try it in the web app). FES 2025 publishes figures from 2024, so these two weather years are all a FES system can be scaled from today; the Ten Year Outlook, which has no 2024 wind, is left out.

    The pathways with no shortfall first. GBP per MW-year, mean of the two weather years:

    Battery fleet (× FES 2035) 0 0.5 1 2 3
    Falling Behind: fleet GW 0 11.3 22.7 45.3 68.0
    Falling Behind: one more battery earns 43,609 21,840 15,551 10,362 8,017
    Falling Behind: daily spread (£/MWh) 72.2 47.9 40.6 32.6 28.2
    Ten Year Forecast: fleet GW 0 15.0 29.9 59.9 89.8
    Ten Year Forecast: one more battery earns 40,897 17,196 12,057 8,130 6,296
    Ten Year Forecast: daily spread (£/MWh) 71.9 41.0 34.3 28.1 24.3

    The first half of the FES fleet takes half or more of a battery's value away; the next two and a half fleets take nearly two thirds of what is left. Batteries earn by flattening the daily spread, so each one leaves less spread for the next. The two weather years agree: in Falling Behind a battery joining the FES fleet earns £14,927 on 2024's weather and £16,176 on 2025's.

    The other three pathways are short of firm capacity in a cold, still spell, and the batteries do little about it:

    Pathway Firm capacity added (GW) for a fleet of × 0 / 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 3 Firm capacity each MW of the FES fleet replaces (MW)
    Holistic Transition 11.25 / 9.25 / 8.25 / 8.0 / 8.25 0.10
    Electric Engagement 11.25 / 8.75 / 8.25 / 7.25 / 7.25 0.09
    Hydrogen Evolution 8.75 / 6.75 / 4.75 / 4.0 / 3.0 0.16

    The FES fleets store under an hour and a half on average, and the shortfalls they would cover last longer: the first half of the fleet saves 2.0 to 2.5 GW of firm capacity, and the next two and a half fleets 1.0 to 3.75 GW more. In 250 MW steps the search is not perfectly monotonic (Holistic Transition needs 8.0 GW at twice the fleet and 8.25 GW at three times). In these pathways a battery's value also rests on a handful of scarcity days: a 1 MW battery with the FES fleet earns £16,591 in Holistic Transition, £17,441 in Electric Engagement and £26,961 in Hydrogen Evolution, of which 23%, 17% and 45% comes on the days with an hour priced at £1,000/MWh or more (one to three across the two weather years). Holistic Transition's and Electric Engagement's are all in 2025's weather; a value resting on them is a statement about one cold week, not about the pathway.

    What this says, with its limits: in a GB system that has enough firm capacity, battery value halves or more as the first 11 to 15 GW of FES's fleet arrives, and falls to 15–18% of the first battery's value at three times the fleet. It is the perfect-foresight value of a 2-hour battery on a model whose daily spread is 76% of the real one in 2025, so the levels are lower bounds on what the spread allows and upper bounds on what a forecast captures; the shape of the curve is the result.

    Limits¶

    • One GB bus: no constraint between Scotland and England, which is where much FES curtailment happens.
    • Links take the neighbours' actual prices for the weather year, so neighbours do not respond to GB, and their 2030s fleets are not modelled. New links are not added as new neighbours; FES interconnector growth scales the existing ten.
    • Capacities from the register are today's; earlier limits on Moyle, IFA and BritNed are not modelled separately, only through each link's fitted availability (see Data).
    • Each link's availability is one number per direction for every hour and year (with availability: gap, one per price-gap bin); new FES capacity gets the existing links' shares.
    • Ember gives one price for Norway and one for Denmark; GB's links land in southern Norway and western Denmark.
    • The weather year's fuel and carbon prices are kept; storage efficiencies for FES batteries and long-duration storage are stated assumptions.
    • With unit commitment (the published scenarios), storage is planned one day at a time seeing 24 hours ahead; without it, with perfect foresight over the year.

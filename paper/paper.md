# Summary

OpenEnergy values grid batteries on Great Britain's (GB) electricity market from open data
alone. A user describes a battery and a forecasting approach in a short YAML file;
OpenEnergy plans every day at the time a real operator would have to commit, using only
what was published by then, settles the plan at the prices that actually cleared, and
reports revenue, wear and the share of the perfect-foresight value captured. The same
engine values frequency-response services alongside day-ahead trading, co-located solar
and wind, and batteries on prices from a cost-based model of GB's power plants, which can
be run on NESO's Future Energy Scenarios with interconnectors trading at neighbouring
markets' prices. Every bundled dataset carries its licence and attribution into each
result. A web application runs the battery optimiser in the browser
(HiGHS compiled to WebAssembly) on any day since 2019.

# Statement of need

Questions about storage in GB are usually answered with commercial price forecasts and
proprietary models, so their results cannot be checked. Open energy-system models such as
PyPSA [@brown2018] optimise investment and dispatch but do not backtest an operator's
day-ahead decisions without lookahead, and open price data such as Open Power System Data
[@opsd2020] stops in 2020 for GB. OpenEnergy fills that gap for researchers and students:
it is strict about the information available at each decision (a test checks that no
forecast uses a value published after its decision time), reports what it cannot model
instead of fitting it to prices, and ships every input with its source so any number can
be reproduced from the repository.

# Functionality

- Battery physics with efficiencies, state-of-charge limits and ageing; a daily
  mixed-integer dispatch solved with HiGHS [@huangfu2018], optionally planning several
  days ahead.
- Forecasters from last week's prices to a gradient-boosted model [@pedregosa2011] trained
  each month on what was known the day before: NESO's day-ahead demand forecast, Elexon's
  day-ahead wind forecast, the daily gas price and the auction prices already cleared.
- Revenue stacking with GB's dynamic frequency-response services, using auction prices and
  delivery derived from system frequency.
- A cost-based model of GB's thermal fleet (merit order or unit commitment) driven by
  DUKES capacities, daily gas and carbon prices, checked against 2015–2025 prices and fuel
  use; NESO Future Energy Scenarios on a historic weather year, made secure to GB's
  reliability standard, with price-taking interconnector links fitted to actual flows.
- A storage saturation study that measures how a battery's value falls as the national
  fleet grows, and a Gymnasium [@towers2024] environment that tests reinforcement-learning
  agents against the optimiser on the same information.
- A command line, a Python API, interactive HTML reports and a React web application.

# Research use

Results in the documentation are regenerated by the scripts and commands next to them.
They include how much of a battery's perfect-foresight value simple and fundamentals-based
forecasts capture in each year from 2017 to 2026; the value of frequency response; that a
plan over price scenarios equals a plan on their mean for day-ahead trading; how far a
cost-based model explains GB prices through the 2021–2022 gas crisis; and that a battery
joining NESO's own 2035 fleet earns about a third of what the first battery would on a
secure system.

# OpenEnergy v2.2 — Design

Status: released · Tag: `v2.2.0`

v2.2 moves from single sites to the GB system: how renewables reshape net load, when they
create surplus, how much storage that surplus calls for, and what storage and renewables
mean for emissions.

## Goals

- Describe net load (load minus wind and solar) and its ramps, today and with renewables
  scaled up, and relate price to net load.
- Quantify system surplus above a must-run floor and the storage needed to absorb it.
- Size storage for a site by sweeping any scenario setting.
- Account for emissions with GB carbon intensity, and let dispatch respond to a carbon price.

## Non-goals (v2.2)

Marginal emission factors (not published for GB), network constraints, unit commitment and
merit-order dispatch (v3.0), regional analysis.

## Data

**Carbon intensity.** NESO's Carbon Intensity API (CC BY 4.0) publishes national
half-hourly intensity in gCO2/kWh: NESO's day-ahead `forecast` and estimated `actual`.
`scripts/fetch_carbon_intensity.py` downloads 2018-01-01 to 2020-09-30 in 14-day windows
and writes `data/carbon_intensity/gb_national.csv` (`utc_timestamp`, `forecast`, `actual`).
`CarbonIntensitySource` loads it as validated series, averaged to the price grid.

The bundled file is kept exactly as published. It has 48,192 half-hours with no timestamp
gaps, but 438 missing `actual` values (nine runs, the longest about three days) and 16
impossible `forecast` values up to 13,579 g. The loader treats values outside
[0, 1000] gCO2/kWh (above any GB fuel's factor; coal is about 937) as missing and reports
how many; short gaps follow the usual `max_gap_hours` interpolation.

**Load and generation.** `OPSDCsvSource.series(column)` returns any OPSD column as a
validated series; GB load and actual wind and solar generation are in the bundled extract.
ENTSO-E's GB actual load is total demand (it peaks at midday in summer, with no solar
dip), so subtracting solar and wind does not double count.

The load series contains telemetry glitches (31 hours below 12 GW, down to 511 MW, and
one- to two-hour drops to half of demand) that create false negative net load and 30-40 GW
"ramps". `flag_glitches` removes load readings that deviate from their centred 5-hour
median by more than 25% of the 99th percentile, or fall below half the 7-day median
(133 of 50,394 hours). Wind and solar are left unchanged. Ramps are reported as 99th
percentiles so any remaining bad reading cannot set them.

## Net load and surplus

For load $L_t$, wind $W_t$, solar $S_t$ and scale factors $k_w, k_s$:

$$N_t = L_t - k_w W_t - k_s S_t$$

Per year: renewable share, mean, peak and minimum net load, largest 1-hour and 3-hour ramps,
the net-load duration curve, and the least-squares slope of price on net load.

**Surplus** with a must-run floor $F$ (inflexible generation that cannot turn down):
$U_t = \max(0, F - N_t)$. Total surplus energy and surplus hours are reported per year.
`openenergy netload DATA [--scale-wind] [--scale-solar] [--must-run]` prints this.

## Storage sizing

**System.** A fleet of power $P$ and duration $h$ (energy $P h$) charges from surplus and
discharges when net load is above the floor, greedily in time order with round-trip
efficiency. Reported: share of surplus absorbed, energy released, equivalent cycles.
A grid over $P$ and $h$ shows diminishing returns.

**Site.** `openenergy sweep SCENARIO --set key=v1,v2 ...` runs a scenario over the
Cartesian product of dotted-key values (e.g. `battery.energy_mwh=1,2,4`) and ranks results.

## Emissions

With intensity $e_t$ in gCO2/kWh (= kg/MWh):

- Battery or site net emissions: $-\sum_t e_t\, x_t\, \Delta t / 1000$ tCO2 for net export
  $x_t$ (MW). Export displaces grid generation; import adds to it.
- Plant avoided emissions: $\sum_t e_t\, y_t\, \Delta t / 1000$ tCO2 for plant output
  delivered to the grid.

**Carbon-aware dispatch.** `carbon.price_per_t` $\lambda$ adds $\lambda \hat e_t / 1000$
to the planning price, so charging is penalised when the grid is carbon-intensive. Money
results stay market revenue; the carbon term only steers dispatch.

The planned intensity $\hat e_t$ is, by default, the actual intensity of the same interval
a week earlier (two weeks if missing), using only data before the decision time, exactly
like `NaiveLastWeek` for prices. `planning: actual` uses the real values as an explicit
upper bound. NESO's archived `forecast` is **not** used for planning: it is the last
forecast issued before each half-hour, not the one available the day before. Planning on
it at noon the day before raised a battery's 2019 revenue as the carbon price rose
(£13.0k to £13.6k at £200/t), which a cost can only do by leaking information; with
last-week intensity, revenue falls as expected.

**Caveat.** These are average intensities. Storage changes the marginal plant, whose
emissions can differ from the average; results show direction and rough scale, not causal
impact. This is stated in outputs and docs.

## Testing

- Carbon data loading, resampling, attribution; generic OPSD columns.
- Net load and surplus on hand-computed series; scaling; ramps; price slope.
- Greedy storage: energy balance, never exceeds surplus or capacity, efficiency losses.
- Emissions accounting by hand; carbon price shifts charging to low-intensity hours.
- `sweep` parsing (types, errors), ranking and CLI smoke tests.

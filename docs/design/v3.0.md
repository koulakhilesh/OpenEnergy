# OpenEnergy v3.0 — Design

Status: approved 2026-10-10, in progress

v3.0 models the GB power system as a whole: which plants run each hour, what that costs,
and the price it implies. It first rebuilds 2015–2020 from public data and checks the
result against what actually happened, then uses that tested base for what-if questions:
more wind and solar, coal retired, more storage, different gas or carbon prices. Modelled
prices feed back into the v2.x battery and plant backtests, so a battery can be valued in
a system that already has many batteries.

## Decisions taken (review these first)

The design questions went unanswered, so these were decided on the stated grounds. Each
can be changed before implementation starts.

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Purpose | **Backcast first**, then scenarios on the tested base | A model never checked against history gives numbers nobody can trust; the bundled data already covers 2015–2020 |
| 2 | Detail | **Linear economic dispatch** with efficiency tranches; no unit commitment | Captures the merit order and storage; start-up costs and minimum stable output add integer variables, much slower solves and many unpublished parameters. Candidate for v3.1 |
| 3 | PyPSA | **Optional extra** `openenergy[system]` behind an adapter | PyPSA 1.4 needs ~840 MB of dependencies (geopandas, netCDF, plotly); battery users should not pay for it |
| 4 | Demand | **NESO total generation** for the system model | It is the sum of the same fuel columns used for validation, so supply and demand are consistent by construction |
| 5 | Imports, nuclear, biomass, hydro | **Fixed at historic output** in the backcast | No public data to model continental prices or plant-level constraints; scenarios can scale them |
| 6 | Storage horizon | **Perfect foresight within each solve window** | Standard for system models; stated as an idealised benchmark, unlike the no-lookahead backtests |
| 7 | Scope | **GB, one bus** | NESO data is GB-wide; network constraints are a later release |

## Goals

- Rebuild hourly GB dispatch and price for 2015-04 to 2020-09 from fleet, fuel and carbon
  data, and report how far it is from real prices and the real gas/coal split. (HMRC
  publishes per-fuel carbon price support rates from 1 April 2015.)
- Run scenarios on that base: scale renewables, change fleet capacity, fuel and carbon
  prices, and add storage; report price, generation mix, curtailment, unserved energy,
  emissions and cost.
- Feed modelled prices into `run_backtest` to value storage in the system it changes.

## Non-goals (v3.0)

Unit commitment (start-up costs, minimum stable generation, ramp limits), network
constraints and zonal or nodal models, interconnector and continental market modelling,
capacity expansion (choosing what to build), the balancing mechanism, years after 2020
(the bundled price data ends 2020-09-30).

## Data

Every bundled file gets a `data/<folder>/README.md` (source, licence, changes made, known
issues, non-endorsement), an attribution constant in code, a fetch script that reproduces
it, and entries in `docs/data.md`, `README.md` and `summary.json`, as in v2.2.

**Generation mix (validation and fixed profiles).** NESO *Historic generation mix and
carbon intensity*, half-hourly GB generation in MW by fuel from 2009: `GAS`, `COAL`,
`NUCLEAR`, `WIND`, `WIND_EMB`, `SOLAR`, `HYDRO`, `BIOMASS`, `IMPORTS`, `STORAGE`, `OTHER`
and their total `GENERATION`. Licence: NESO Open Data Licence v1.0 (based on OGL v3);
attribution must read "Supported by National Energy SO Open Data", and must not imply
endorsement. `scripts/fetch_system_data.py` downloads it from the NESO API and keeps
2015-01-01 to 2020-09-30 and the columns above, unchanged in value (all whole MW). Known
issues to document: NESO applies seasonal decomposition to fill gaps; net-negative values
(pumping, net exports) are set to zero, so `STORAGE` is net discharge only and `IMPORTS`
never negative; transmission solar and batteries sit in `OTHER`. Timestamps are UTC period
starts: every day has 48 half-hours including clock-change days, and the solar peak sits
at 11:30-12:00 UTC in both June and December.

**Fleet capacity.** DUKES 5.8, *Major power producers' plant capacity* (England and Wales,
Scotland, Northern Ireland), end-of-year MW; GB = England and Wales + Scotland. Coal and
nuclear from table 5.8.A (by fuel); CCGT, gas turbines and oil engines from 5.8.C (by
technology). OGL v3. Gas turbines and oil engines form one peaking group.

**Fleet efficiency.** DUKES 5.10.C, average thermal efficiency by type (gross calorific
value): CCGT about 46–47% and coal about 36% over 2015–2020. OGL v3.

**Fuel prices.** DESNZ *Quarterly Energy Prices* table 3.2.1: average prices of coal and
gas bought by the major UK power producers, pence per kWh of fuel, excluding the Carbon
Price Support levy. Quarterly, cash terms. OGL v3. Quarterly prices miss daily gas
volatility; this is a stated limitation (daily hub prices are not openly licensed).

**Carbon price.** Two parts, each changing on 1 April:

- Carbon Price Support, HMRC Excise Notice CCL1/6 (OGL): per-fuel rates, gas £/kWh and
  coal £/GJ (gross CV), for 2015/16 and from 2016/17. Dividing the 2016 rates by their
  £18/tCO2 basis gives the emission factors used throughout: gas 0.1839 and coal
  0.3096 tCO2 per MWh of fuel.
- EU ETS allowance price on 1 April each year, World Bank Carbon Pricing Dashboard
  (CC BY 4.0), in US$, converted with the ECB's EUR reference rates for USD and GBP on that
  day (or the last business day before). The dashboard blocks scripted downloads, so the
  six published values are recorded in the fetch script. One price per year misses moves
  within the year (2018 rose from about EUR 10 to 25); this is a stated limitation.

**Prices for validation.** The bundled OPSD GB day-ahead price (2015 to 2020-09).

All of this is written to `data/system/` by `scripts/fetch_system_data.py`; only small
tidy CSVs are extracted from the DUKES and QEP workbooks.

## Model

Hourly snapshots on one GB bus (NESO half-hours averaged to hours, on the OPSD price grid).

**Demand** $D_t$ = NESO `GENERATION`. Supply must equal demand every hour.

**Fixed profiles.** Nuclear, biomass, hydro, imports, storage (historic net discharge) and
other are generators fixed at their historic output, each optionally scaled in scenarios.

**Wind and solar.** Generators with zero marginal cost and availability equal to historic
output (transmission + embedded wind, solar) times a scale factor. They can be curtailed
when supply exceeds demand; curtailment is reported.

**Thermal fleet.** For group $g$ (CCGT, coal, peaking), capacity $C_{g,t}$ (DUKES 5.8,
interpolated linearly between year ends) times availability $a$ is split into $K$ equal
tranches whose efficiencies spread evenly over $\bar\eta_{g,y} \pm \delta_g$, with
$\bar\eta_{g,y}$ from DUKES 5.10.C. The short-run marginal cost of tranche $k$ is

$$c_{k,t} = \frac{p_{f,q(t)} + s_{f,t} + \lambda_t\, e_f}{\eta_k} + v_g$$

with fuel price $p$ (£/MWh of fuel, by quarter $q$), carbon price support $s$ (£/MWh of
fuel), EU ETS price $\lambda$ (£/t), emission factor $e_f$ (t/MWh of fuel) and variable
O&M $v_g$. Defaults: $K = 5$, $a = 0.9$, $\delta_{\text{CCGT}} = 4$ pp,
$\delta_{\text{coal}} = 2$ pp, peaking costed as gas at 35% efficiency, $v_g = 0$ (no open
source; configurable). These are stated, configurable assumptions; none is fitted to
prices. Observed peak gas output is about 88% of CCGT plus gas-turbine capacity in every
year, which supports $a = 0.9$.

A throwaway prototype of this merit order (no storage, defaults above) gave, against the
OPSD day-ahead price: 2019 mean £42.3 vs £42.9/MWh, hourly correlation 0.63; 2016-2018
means £4-7 low. It ran far less coal than happened (2016: 9.6 vs 28.0 TWh) and its 95th
percentile price was £15-22 below actual. Both are expected limits of a cost-only model
and are reported, not tuned away.

**Storage.** Scenario storage (power, duration, round-trip efficiency) is optimised in
the dispatch. In the backcast, historic pumped storage stays a fixed profile, because
NESO's data omits pumping demand and modelling it would break the demand balance.

**Unserved energy.** A load-shedding generator at the value of lost load (default
£6,000/MWh) keeps every solve feasible; any use is reported.

**Price.** The hourly marginal price of the demand balance (the cost of one more MWh).
Without storage it equals the marginal cost of the most expensive running tranche; with
storage it also reflects the value of stored energy.

**Solve window.** One linear programme per year, storage cyclic over the year. A
one-year, one-bus test network with pumped storage solved in about 2 s with HiGHS, so
splitting into shorter windows is not needed.

## Architecture

```
openenergy/data/neso.py        GenerationMixSource: load, validate, attribution
openenergy/data/fleet.py       capacity, efficiency, fuel and carbon price tables
openenergy/system/fleet.py     tranches and marginal costs (pure, no PyPSA)
openenergy/system/model.py     SystemSpec -> SystemResult; backend choice
openenergy/system/_pypsa.py    the only module that imports PyPSA
openenergy/system/merit.py     pure numpy merit order (no storage)
openenergy/system/validate.py  modelled vs actual price and fuel mix
```

`SystemSpec` (pydantic) holds demand, fixed profiles, renewables, tranches, storage and
VOLL, all as validated UTC series. `solve(spec)` returns `SystemResult`: price, generation
by technology, curtailment, unserved energy, emissions and total cost. PyPSA is imported
lazily; without the extra, a clear `ConfigError` names the install command.

The pure merit order is an independent check: with no storage, PyPSA's price and dispatch
must match it exactly. It also lets most tests run without PyPSA. Without storage every
period is independent, so the merit order *is* the optimum and `solve(spec)` uses it
(`backend="auto"`); PyPSA is used when storage links periods. On 2019 data both backends
give identical prices and costs, hourly and half-hourly; a year with storage solves in
under 2 s through the LP-file route (the direct API prints the HiGHS banner before
options apply).

**Link to backtests.** `SystemResult.price` is a `PriceSeries`, so `run_backtest` can
value a battery on modelled prices with its usual no-lookahead forecasts. A sweep over
added system storage then shows how each battery's revenue falls as the fleet grows.

## Validation

For each year: modelled vs actual hourly price (mean, bias, MAE, correlation, and the
price duration curve), modelled vs actual gas and coal generation (TWh and share), and
modelled vs NESO emissions where intensity data exists (2018–2020). No parameter is
fitted to the prices; the gap is reported as a finding. Expected, to be confirmed:
a cost-based model underestimates price peaks (scarcity, start-up costs, bidding above
cost) and smooths within-quarter gas price moves.

## Interfaces

**Scenario file**, a new `system:` section (battery scenarios unchanged):

```yaml
system:
  year: 2019
  scale: {wind: 2.0, solar: 2.0, nuclear: 1.0}
  capacity_mw: {coal: 0}
  carbon_price_per_t: 60
  storage: [{power_mw: 5000, hours: 4, efficiency: 0.85}]
```

**CLI:** `openenergy system run SCENARIO`, `openenergy system validate --year 2019`.
`openenergy sweep` accepts system scenarios through the same dotted keys.

**Outputs:** `summary.json` (with all data attributions and the perfect-foresight note),
hourly `dispatch.csv`, and `validation.csv` for backcasts.

## Testing

- Loaders: column checks, gaps, attribution text, timestamp convention.
- Tranches and marginal costs computed by hand; carbon and fuel price effects.
- Merit order on small hand-built systems (price steps, curtailment, shedding).
- PyPSA backend equals merit order without storage; storage only shifts energy from
  cheap to dear hours; energy balance holds every hour. Marked so they skip without
  the extra, and run in CI (which installs all extras).
- Validation metrics on synthetic series; CLI smoke tests; example scenarios load.
